# What's New in 1.0.70

**Spotter Tools Pro  |  iOS, Android and Windows**

![](/images/whats-new-1.0.70.jpg)

**This one is about speed.** Radar starts sooner, archive loops build in a fraction of the time, 3D storms paint almost immediately because the volume is built for you now, and the Windows map finally draws at the rate your monitor runs. Everything below ships on all three platforms unless a section says otherwise.

## Radar gets going faster

- **Archive loops load roughly a third faster, and start playing sooner.** On a cold two hour Level 2 loop the wait for a fully built loop dropped from about two and a half minutes to about a minute and forty, and the first frame you can actually play arrives about ten seconds sooner.
- **Loops only download the part of the file they need.** A Level 2 volume is a big file and a loop frame paints one tilt of it, so the app now stops reading once it has that tilt. About two thirds of the bytes on a typical file, and as little as a quarter on some.
- **And only decodes the tilt it paints.** It used to unpack every cut below your elevation to draw one of them, which is most of the work in a loop frame.
- **Faster machines are actually used.** Desktops spread archive decoding across more cores, and six core phones get a second decode worker instead of grinding through a loop on one.
- **Phones with memory to spare keep more frames.** The loop budget used to be one flat number sized for a mid range phone, so a 12 GB phone would thin a two hour loop down to thirteen frames with gigabytes going spare. It now scales with your device, and a large phone keeps roughly twice the loop it did.
- **Opening a site paints the real radar sooner on phones.** The first image you saw was often a Level III picture up to a minute older than the Level 2 data already sitting there waiting. The newer data now takes the screen.

## Storms in 3D, built for you

The 3D storm volume arrived in 1.0.69 and your device had to build it. Now the servers build it and send it down ready to draw.

- **It appears almost immediately**, rather than after your phone has ground through a full scan. If a prepared volume is not available for your site, or it is stale, the app quietly falls back to building it locally as before.
- **Site 3D and national 3D have their own opacity sliders**, so you can leave the mosaic sheer for context and keep your own radar solid, or the reverse.
- **Less banding inside the volume**, so a storm reads as a solid body rather than a stack of visible shells.

> **Debris and rotation still need the full local scan**, so on a fresh site the storm itself can show up before its highlights do.

## The map turns when you mean it

Two finger twist arrived last release and it was too easy to trigger: a pinch with any wobble in it would leave the map slightly turned.

- **A turn now takes a deliberate twist** of about twelve degrees. Under that, the map treats the gesture as the pinch or drag you meant.
- **Nothing jumps when it does engage.** The movement you spent crossing the threshold is absorbed rather than applied all at once.
- **Lifting one finger can no longer turn a zoom into a rotation.**
- **There is a lock button** if you would rather the map never turned at all.
- **The heading readout stays honest** while rotation is locked.

> **The detent at north is unchanged, and Chaser Mode still locks north up.**

## Weather alerts

- **SPC outlook notifications are about you now.** They were firing on the highest risk anywhere in the country, so a moderate risk in Nebraska would send a Tennessee user a banner quoting Nebraska's numbers, and tapping it showed no outlook anywhere on the map. Outlooks are now matched against your own watch zones, the alert describes the risk over each of your zones, and tapping it turns the outlook layer on and flies you to the strongest one.
- **A nationwide watch zone no longer means "the whole country" for outlooks.** It still works exactly as before for warnings; there is a new setting if you want the old behaviour back.
- **No more outlooks that already expired** arriving after the app was restarted in the background.

## Forecast

- **Every forecast period carries its date.** The rail cards show "Sep 16" under the day name, and the selected period and the full list spell it out.
- **Wind is on the rail cards**, not only inside the detail.
- **A rising or falling temperature arrow** when the forecast office flags one.
- **Rain chance is always drawn**, so a dry day between two wet ones reads as zero rather than as a missing line.
- **The hourly strip labels the day** at the start and at every midnight.

## Cameras

**Nothing here needs this update.** Camera coverage is pushed from our servers, and it has grown a great deal since the summer. If you have not looked at the camera layer lately it has far more in it than it did.

- **Ten more states came online** (Alaska, Connecticut, Delaware, Idaho, Maine, Massachusetts, Nevada, New Hampshire, Oregon and Vermont), about 3,700 cameras, nearly a thousand of them live video.
- **Kentucky Mesonet's 78 weather stations** joined, along with 22 more live Kentucky feeds.
- **The curated live list grew from 261 cameras to 402**, including a seventy six camera network across sixteen states that had only ever been visible as still images, and Arizona going from four live cameras to fifty three.
- **Rotated streams repair themselves.** Some operators restart their broadcast daily, which used to leave a dead camera until someone noticed. A job now checks the whole roster every fifteen minutes and repoints anything that moved.

What this release adds is the fix for the freeze that could come with all those cameras, below, and the same push mechanism for the Live Chasers list, so chasers can be added or retired without an update from now on.

## Fixes

- **A frozen map when cameras were on.** Drawing a few thousand cameras happened on the same thread as the interface, and on Android it could stop the app long enough for the system to offer to close it. Camera drawing now happens off to the side, one pass at a time, and pauses in the background.
- **A red error box in Settings.** Four sliders could paint an error panel over the section they were in; Map and Radar's Radar Overlay section was the one people hit.
- **Radar could get stuck after switching products** with two views open, or leave a worker running after you left the screen.
- **A blank map at the middle of the ocean** on a first launch with no location permission and no home set. It now opens on the country.

## Specific to Windows

- **The map draws at your monitor's rate.** It was held to 55 frames a second no matter what your display could do, by a capture setting we did not know was being applied. Measured 55 to 154 frames a second on a 165 Hz panel.
- **The pan is even**, not three frames held then four.
- **Clicking the map no longer dims the title bar**, and no longer costs you a click. The window was being deactivated by every click on the map, and any click that landed during the bounce back was swallowed.
- **After tilting the map, the next click works.** A right drag tilt used to eat exactly one click.
- **Desktop shortcuts and clicks work after minimising** or hiding to the tray.

> **If you are on a 60 Hz monitor you were always getting the full 60**, so the frame rate change is not something you will see. Everything else on this list applies to every PC.
