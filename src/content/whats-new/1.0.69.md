# What's New in 1.0.69

**Spotter Tools Pro  |  iOS, Android and Windows**

![](/images/whats-new-1.0.69.jpg)

**Two big things this release.** You can stand a storm up in three dimensions and look inside it, and the map turns now. Everything below ships on all three platforms unless a section says otherwise.

## Storms in 3D

Radar has always been a flat slice. This release stands the whole scan up as a solid you can fly around, so you can see how a storm is built rather than inferring it from one tilt at a time.

### Two ways in, and they follow the layer you are on

- **Your radar site's own volume.** Every tilt of a Level 2 scan resampled into a single block, so you get one storm in full detail.
- **The nationwide mosaic.** Works anywhere in the lower 48 at about a two minute cadence, so you are not tied to one radar's umbrella.

### What you can actually see in it

- **Debris and rotation are marked inside the storm.** Debris shows where the radar sees something that is not rain, and rotation shows as a column through the storm rather than a couplet you have to find on one tilt.
- **Cores show through the envelope.** Opacity follows rain rate, so heavy cores stay solid while light rain goes sheer and you can see into the storm from any side, the way the flat view shows it from below.
- **Height is yours to set**, including true scale if you want the storm at its real proportions rather than exaggerated.
- **It plays.** The volume runs with the loop, so you can watch a storm build and collapse in three dimensions instead of stepping through stills.

> **A few honest notes.** The 3D chip appears when your device can render it, so it will not be on every phone. It always starts off when you open the app, on purpose, so you never get a pitched 3D map and a heavy load you did not ask for. And it renders at whatever resolution keeps your device smooth, dropping detail before it drops frames.

## The map turns

Turning the map used to be Chaser Mode only on mobile, and on Windows it was not offered at all.

- **On phones and tablets**, twist with two fingers to turn. Tilt is still the two finger vertical drag it always was.
- **On Windows**, hold the right mouse button and drag: sideways turns, up and down tilts. On a trackpad, ctrl and drag with the left button.
- **There is a detent at north.** Come back within a few degrees and the map snaps to exactly north and stays there, so a twist that was really a pinch cannot leave you sitting three degrees off. Turning away takes a deliberate push, and the turn you put in while it was held is paid back in one motion rather than thrown away.

> **Chaser Mode still locks north up**, because it re-centres on every fix and a turnable map there would fight you the whole drive.

## Lighter on memory

The satellite layer was the single heaviest thing in the app, and four separate places were holding full resolution imagery with nothing on screen that could show it. All four now let go. If you run satellite alongside radar, especially on a phone, the app has a great deal more headroom than it did.

## Fixes

- **The 3D chips no longer come back on by themselves.** They shared saved settings with the shape controls, so a session that ended in 3D handed the next launch a pitched 3D map at cold start. Your height and floor choices are still remembered.
- **Turning on 3D from a Level III product now switches you to the Level 2 twin** of what you were looking at, rather than doing nothing, since a volume needs the full scan.
- **Exported loops carry the 3D volume**, so what you share is what you saw.
- **On iPad, the mosaic volume repainted slowly** and now keeps up.

## Specific to Windows

- **Right-drag to orbit** replaces the old two button tilt drag, which nobody found. Sideways turns and up or down tilts, with proper inertia when you let go. Ctrl and left-drag does the same on a trackpad, and a touchscreen gets twist and two finger tilt.
- Ending an orbit no longer opens the long press menu by accident.
