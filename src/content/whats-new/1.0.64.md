# What's New in 1.0.64

**Spotter Tools Pro  |  iOS, Android and Windows**

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539547818-img01.webp)

**Spotter Tools Pro 1.0.64 is out on iOS, Android and Windows.** Hurricane Hunter aircraft are on the map, storm reports moved to a fresher and much wider source, Wind Flow got real controls, and the Level 2 problem some of you hit last month is fixed. Everything below ships on all three platforms unless a section says otherwise.

## Hurricane Hunters, live on the map

A new **Aviation** layer carrying live NOAA and Air Force reconnaissance: flight level observations along the track, and the vortex centre fixes the crews send back from the eye.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539548908-img02.webp)

- **Tap any flight** for the aircraft, the mission, and what it is measuring. A live badge tells you whether it is still reporting or the track is finished.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539549803-img03.webp)

- **A Recon Graphs screen** plots surface wind against flight level wind with the tropical storm and hurricane force bands marked, then pressure. The centre fix tab charts inbound against outbound maxima, minimum pressure, eye temperature with dew point, and standard level height.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539550747-img04.webp)

- **Gaps are drawn as gaps.** A missing bulletin leaves a hole in the trace rather than a straight line pretending the data was there, and where a field is genuinely absent the chart says which quantity it fell back to instead of drawing something else under the same title.
- **It is its own layer, not part of Tropical, on purpose.** About half of all recon flights are synoptic surveillance or training with no storm involved, so a training flight over New Mexico appearing under a Tropical toggle would read as a bug.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539551657-img05.webp)

## Storm reports, from a much better source

The old SPC feed was a once-a-day digest covering tornado, hail and damaging wind, and it lagged. It has been replaced with **NWS Local Storm Reports** straight from the offices that issue them.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539553119-img06.webp)

- **Minutes fresh** instead of hours.
- **The full taxonomy**, about 27 report types mapped onto 19 categories, with seven filter chips so you can narrow to what you care about. Anything the NWS sends that we have not seen before still renders rather than being dropped.
- **Report times are now your local clock**, in both the info sheet and the notifications, instead of UTC.
- **Reports stay for 4 hours by default**, up from 1. If you already chose a window, yours is kept.
- The icon size slider and the retention setting moved out of Settings and into the Storm Reports options sheet, so the layer is configured where you turn it on. The whole report icon set was redrawn onto one badge design.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539554213-img07.webp)

## Wind Flow, with actual controls

The Wind Flow row opens an options sheet instead of being just a toggle: **density, size, speed and opacity**. Density, speed and opacity were already live in the engine and simply had nothing driving them; size is new and scales the whole comet, head and trail together, without touching trail length.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539555279-img08.webp)

**Wind now draws on top of everything.** It used to sit under roads, labels, alert fills and every data overlay. That was requested, and the trade is real: particles now also draw over city labels, warning outlines, spotter and camera icons, and your sketches.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539556269-img09.webp)

## Alert polygons are shaded again

Warnings and watches now fill at 25 percent, and outlooks at 60, where before they were outlines only on most platforms.

That is possible because of what changed underneath: **fills now paint under the radar and outlines paint over it.** Previously the whole polygon sat above the radar, so a translucent fill washed out the echoes, which is the one thing you most need to read inside a warning. Now the fill tints the echoes and the coloured border, casing and emergency dashes stay on top where you can see them.

Mesoscale discussions deliberately stay unfilled by default, because they cover far more ground than a warning and shading them would wash out most of the screen. If you had already picked your own opacity, it is untouched.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539557875-img10.webp)

## Cameras look better and crowd less

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539558955-img11.webp)

- **New marker art**: an amber video badge for live feeds and a blue-grey photo badge for stills, each with a pointer tail that rotates to the direction the camera faces.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539560527-img12.webp)

- **Cluster bubbles are smaller and translucent** instead of solid discs, and both markers and bubbles now follow one icon size slider.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539561815-img13.webp)

- **A new Group nearby cameras switch.** Turn it off and dense metros show individual cameras as space allows rather than one smeared cluster.
- The camera sources sheet gained a Display section and split the source list into State DOT and Traffic against Scenic and Specialty, each with a count of what is on.

## National radar gets its own controls

The composite smoothing chip is replaced by a **National Radar sub-sheet** with a continuous **Smoothness** slider and its own **Opacity** slider, split from the single site value so the two layers no longer share one number.

Smoothness now drives the whole pipeline rather than being a switch, so at 0 you see the real grid as hard cells and at full you get a smooth resample. The underlying data path is used unconditionally for the reflectivity mosaics now, so low smoothness no longer falls back to blurry pre-coloured tiles. Resolution follows the camera, and loop frames are cached so a replay lands instantly.

![](https://models.dgwaynes.com/announcements/img/1788539563198-img14.webp)

## Fixes

- **Level 2 radar could stop loading entirely.** On 1.0.62 some users found Level 2 reflectivity and velocity both dead while Level III kept working, with a force quit the only cure. A decompression helper could wedge with nothing watching it, and once every helper was stuck, every Level 2 decode queued forever. Nothing appeared in crash reports because a request that never finishes never fails. Helpers are now watched, replaced when they stop answering, and the same protection was applied one level up to the radar worker. The failures are also counted now, so this class of problem cannot go unnoticed again.
- **Echo Tops loops always failed to load.** Every other Level III product looped fine; Echo Tops is built differently and was being rejected by a guard meant for broken data.
- **Tropical shading washed out the radar.** The three translucent tropical fills now paint under the single site radar, with every line, track, dot and label still on top.
- **Deep loops play more smoothly,** and a long standing problem where a loop could draw nothing at all is fixed.

## Specific to Windows

- **The map zoom buttons were under the brand bar.** They shipped at the library's default corner in the very first webview build, which put the plus button beneath the beacon chip, where it was covered and its clicks were being taken. Both buttons now clear the chrome at every window size.
- **Corner popovers collided on short windows.** On a 1080p screen at 175 percent scale the layers panel dropped over the bottom right puck column, and the pucks sat on its last rows and took their clicks. The panel now steps left of the column instead of under it, and the whole corner was audited: the base layer tower and the locate menu were budgeting their height as if the hamburger were free, and the locate menu had no height limit at all and could silently overflow off the top.
- **Turning Wind Flow off killed the single site radar** until you restarted the app. The wind layer left a graphics buffer slot enabled that nothing else reclaimed, so once its buffer was deleted every later radar draw silently painted nothing. Present since Wind Flow first shipped on Windows, not from the new options panel.
- **Every compact loop frame drew nothing.** One missing line meant the graphics driver silently discarded those draws, which is also the real explanation for the old report of loops blinking in and out.
- Camera clustering, camera scale, radar opacity and radar smoothness all gained direct paths so they apply instantly rather than rebuilding the layer.
