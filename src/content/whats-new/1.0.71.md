# What's New in 1.0.71

**Spotter Tools Pro  |  iOS, Android and Windows**

![](/images/whats-new-1.0.71.jpg)

**One of our biggest updates yet.** An experimental tornado detector built right into the radar, a way to fill radar holes with the radars around them, a rain, snow, sleet and ice view of the radar, and nearly every winter and long range outlook NOAA publishes. Everything below ships on all three platforms unless a section says otherwise.

> **New to these features?** There is a step by step guide for each one, for phones, tablets and Windows, at [spottertools.pro/1.0.71/howto](/1.0.71/howto)

## Tornado ID (experimental)

A new option for single site radar that looks at every circulation the radar sees and estimates the chance a tornado is on the ground right now.

- **Built from years of real storms.** It learned from Level 2 radar scans and confirmed tornado reports from the National Weather Service's storm records, and was then tested on a full year of storms it had never seen.
- **A marker with a percentage** sits on each circulation it thinks is worth your attention: amber from 5%, orange from 30% and red from 60%, so the one that matters stands out.
- **Tap a marker** for the Tornado ID card: the score, whether it is rising or falling, how long the circulation has been tracked, how strong the rotation is, whether a debris signature is showing and how high the radar beam is at that range. The storm cell card shows a Tornado ID line too.
- **Works on live radar and in archive mode.** Live scores come from our servers the moment each scan arrives. In archive mode your phone or computer scores the scan itself, so give it a few seconds, and in an archived loop it scores the frame you stop on.
- **Off by default.** Open **Map layers**, tap the **Single Site Radar** row (on Windows, its Options button) and switch on **Tornado ID (experimental)**.

> **Tornado ID is an estimate, not a warning.** It can miss a tornado and it can flag a storm that never produces one. Always follow official National Weather Service warnings and your own eyes.

## Expert Mode: fill the radar holes

One radar can be looking at the top of a storm while the radar on the other side of it sees thousands of feet lower over the same ground. Expert mode puts them together.

- **Up to three radars in one image.** Turn on **Expert** on the radar panel, then tap one or two more radar sites on the map. Tap a site again to take it out.
- **Every pixel is still true single site radar.** Each radar paints only the ground it is closest to, so nothing is counted twice and there is no bright seam where two radars meet.
- **Everything lines up in time.** The app picks the moment all of the radars can cover and fetches each one's scan from then. A radar that has gone down is left out rather than dragging the whole picture back.
- **Numbered badges** show which radars are in the image. The radar you had loaded is number 1, and every other radar on the map dims so you can see what is in play.
- **It starts fresh every time.** Turning Expert off clears it, and it does not come back on its own after a restart.

> **Expert mode works with Level III products at the lowest tilt.** If you switch to a Level II product, a higher tilt, play a loop or turn on 3D, the extra radars step aside and the app tells you why. They come back when you return.

## Precip Type

A new single site radar product that colours the radar as **rain, snow, sleet or freezing rain**.

- **The intensity is the radar, the type is the HRRR model.** Radar cannot see the temperature between the cloud and the ground, so the app reads the precipitation type from the High Resolution Rapid Refresh model and paints each radar pixel on the matching colour scale.
- **Updated every 15 minutes**, so the rain and snow line moves with the storm instead of jumping once an hour. The legend names the model time it is using.
- **The inspector reads the type** at any spot, alongside the reflectivity.
- **Loops work**, with each frame coloured from its own time.

> **Precip Type needs the HRRR model**, which covers the lower 48 states. Radars outside it (Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and Puerto Rico) do not offer it.

## More outlooks than ever

The Outlooks layer now carries nearly everything the Weather Prediction Center and Climate Prediction Center publish.

- **Winter Weather:** the chance of 4, 8 or 12 inches of snow, or a quarter inch of freezing rain, Days 1 to 3.
- **WPC Hazards, Days 3 to 7:** heavy rain, temperature extremes, wildfire risk, soil and flooding hazards, or all of them combined.
- **CPC Hazards, Days 8 to 14:** the week two outlook, including extreme heat and rapid onset drought.
- **Fire Weather out to Day 8.**
- **Rainfall** for Days 4 to 5, Days 6 to 7 and in six hour periods, behind a new Type chip so the menu stays short.
- **Winter Storm Severity parts:** snow amount, snow load, ice and blowing snow, each on its own.
- **Monthly and seasonal** temperature and precipitation outlooks, and the monthly and seasonal drought outlooks beside the Drought Monitor.
- **Faster everywhere.** Outlooks now load from our own servers in a fraction of the time, with NOAA as the backup if our copy is ever unavailable.

## Radar

- **Level 2 velocity is much cleaner.** We rebuilt how the app unfolds velocity and graded it pixel by pixel against the National Weather Service's own Level III product on 43 storms. Patches of wrong colour, the kind that can look like a fake couplet, are about a third as common and flicker far less from scan to scan.
- **The Reflectivity filter now cleans up the national mosaic** as well as single site radar, so light clutter under the filter floor no longer washes across the map.
- **Velocity loops no longer get stuck on one frame** on days when the radar keeps changing scan modes.
- **Archive loops wait for you.** Loading an archive loop now holds on the first frame until you press Play, and returning to live closes the loop and brings the sweep back.
- **Archive radar opens faster** when the time you pick is within the last few hours.
- **Archive radar no longer shows the wrong day** after a network hiccup. A failed lookup used to look exactly like a day with no scans, and the app would quietly reach back to the day before.
- **Live Level 2 is found on busy radars.** A handful of the busiest radars could go unfound for hours because the app stopped reading the list of scans too early.

## Cameras

- **New filter in the Live Cameras sheet:** show **All**, **Live** video only or **Stills** only, with a count of each and of how many are on the map right now.

## Hurricane Hunters

- **Export a flight.** From a flight's graphs, share it as a two page picture brief (the track, wind and pressure charts, the centre fixes and the crew's remarks) or as data files for a spreadsheet.
- **Live tracks recover faster** after the data feed stalls. A stalled flight used to be mistaken for a landed one and checked less often, right when it needed checking more.

## Fixes and polish

- **Soundings open on the hour closest to now**, instead of the model's start time, which was often two hours old.
- **The Tropical options sheet scrolls**, so the storm list and model legend at the bottom are always reachable on a phone.
- **Map layer lists are back in alphabetical order.**

## Specific to Windows

- **Smoother panning on 4K screens and laptop graphics.** Map layers you have not turned on no longer cost anything to draw, which on a typical laptop graphics chip was the difference between stuttering and smooth.
- **New "Scale the whole app" option** in Settings, under Map rendering, for slower PCs on high resolution displays. It renders the whole window at a lower resolution, which makes everything smoother at the cost of slightly softer text. It needs a restart.
- **The Reflectivity filter updates the radar right away.** Moving the slider used to leave the old image on screen until you reloaded the radar.
- **The Layers panel is in alphabetical order**, matching the phone app.

## Nothing here needs this update

These happened on our servers, so everyone already has them:

- **The Level 2 fast lane got much faster.** The newest Level 2 scan now reaches the app about 12 seconds after the radar sends it, down from 40 to 50 seconds.
- **Hurricane Hunter data arrives on time.** One of NOAA's two data feeds was frozen for days and we were only reading that one. Both are read now, and whichever is current wins.
